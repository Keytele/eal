# 02 — EAL Branding

## Brand position

EAL is presented as a premium electric vehicle brand focused on intelligence, calm design, human-centred technology and optimistic progress.

The brand should feel:

- Modern, not futuristic-for-its-own-sake.
- Premium, not ostentatious.
- Technical, not cold.
- Chinese in origin without leaning on stereotypical cultural motifs.
- Global and bilingual.
- Sustainable without greenwashing language.

## Core visual behaviors

- Strong vehicle photography.
- Minimal ornament.
- Horizontal lighting and UI motifs.
- Dark graphite/navy environments.
- Warm architectural lighting.
- Large negative space.
- Thin uppercase typography.
- Fine-line iconography.

## Logo

Use the supplied EAL wordmark/brand mark as the primary navigation mark.

Rules:

- Maintain clear space equal to at least the visual height of one logo stroke around the mark.
- Do not stretch, skew, outline, emboss or add glow.
- Use light logo on dark backgrounds and dark logo on light backgrounds.
- Use monochrome treatment in navigation and footer.
- Vehicle badges shown in photography must match the actual production brand mark.

## Naming system

Vehicle model names are short and alphanumeric:

- C2 — compact urban crossover.
- S4 — premium electric fastback sedan.
- S6 — executive flagship sedan.
- X4 — compact premium SUV.
- X6 — large three-row flagship SUV.
- R9 — halo electric grand tourer.

Use model names exactly as branded. Do not localize the model code itself.

## Product taxonomy

Recommended labels:

- `Sedans`: S4, S6.
- `SUVs`: C2, X4, X6.
- `Grand Tourer`: R9.

If future product strategy classifies the C2 differently, taxonomy must be data-driven.

## Brand voice

### English

Short, controlled, premium, optimistic.

Preferred:

- “A higher standard.”
- “More life ahead.”
- “Technology that moves further.”
- “Designed around intelligence.”

Avoid:

- Overclaiming autonomy.
- Excessive superlatives.
- Hyperactive sales language.
- Meme/slang tone.
- Unverifiable sustainability claims.

### Simplified Chinese

Chinese copy should read naturally as premium Chinese brand copy, not as a literal word-for-word translation.

Principles:

- Favor concise phrase structure.
- Use idiomatic automotive terminology.
- Avoid unnecessary English calques.
- Keep legal/specification terminology precise.
- Preserve model names and trademarked feature names where required.

## Messaging hierarchy

1. Brand promise.
2. Model purpose.
3. Human benefit.
4. Technical proof.
5. Action.

Example:

- Promise: `A higher standard.`
- Model purpose: `Executive electric sedan.`
- Human benefit: `Quiet long-distance comfort.`
- Technical proof: `800V architecture · rear-wheel steering · active noise cancellation.`
- Action: `Configure S6`.

## Feature naming

Use approved feature names consistently across model, technology and commerce pages.

Examples:

- 800V Architecture
- Ultra-Fast Charging
- Highway Pilot Hardware
- Intelligent Driving
- Matrix LED Lighting
- Digital Key
- Remote Parking
- Cabin Software
- OTA Updates
- V2L / V2H

Do not describe driver-assistance features as autonomous driving unless legally approved for the relevant market.

## Sustainability language

Permitted style:

- “Zero tailpipe emissions.”
- “Lower operational emissions” only when supported by lifecycle/market assumptions.
- “Designed for efficient energy use.”
- “Recycled/recovered material content” only with sourced percentages.

Avoid absolute claims such as `zero-emission vehicle` in lifecycle contexts unless legally approved.

## Brand content governance

Every public claim should have one of:

- A structured specification source.
- An approved CMS content owner.
- A legal/compliance approval state.

CMS fields for claims should include:

- Claim text.
- Locale.
- Market.
- Source/reference.
- Approval status.
- Effective date.
- Expiry/review date.
